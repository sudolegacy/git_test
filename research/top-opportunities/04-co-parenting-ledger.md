# 04 — Co-Parenting Ledger with One-Parent Mode (Score 70/100)

**Concept:** co-parenting communication, custody-calendar and expense app priced per family rather than per parent, with a tamper-evident message log, custody calendar that models real orders (alternating weeks, holiday overrides, exceptions), expense ledger with receipts and splits, court-ready PDF exports, **a one-parent mode that works fully even if the other parent never joins**, AI that drafts the calendar from an uploaded custody order *with mandatory human review*, and a tone-check on outgoing messages.

**Pricing:** $49/year per parent or $79/year per family.

Evidence labels: **V** verified (primary/press), **3P** third-party estimate, **U** unverified, **Calc** own calculation. Research date 12 Nov 2026.

## 2. Market

- **US:** families with own children under 18: **33.56M in 2025** (FRED/Census, V); one-parent families with children under 18: **10.24M in 2025** (FRED, V) — an upper bound, since not all are two-household custody cases. Families with own children are 39% of families (Census, V).
- **Captive demand:** courts and lawyers order or recommend co-parenting apps (family-law firm guides, 3P); "the ex won't join" is a recurring theme in competitor guides (3P) — the basis for the one-parent mode.
- **Incumbent scale (3P):** OurFamilyWizard ~$11.6M (LinkedIn) / ~$10.5M (Latka, Oct 2024) revenue, Spectrum Equity investment 2020 (Crunchbase News, V), owns Cozi since May 2022 (PR Newswire, V). TalkingParents "bootstrapped to $10M ARR with 100,000+ paying customers" (promotional, U).
- **Pricing shift in 2026:** TalkingParents free plan ended 30 Mar 2026 (date disputed; one source says 1 Jun) (3P); AppClose free model ended 1 Jan 2026 (3P). Price-sensitive parents are being pushed to look.
- **UK/other:** family counts and divorced co-parent numbers not retrieved (U).

## 3. Customer problem

1. **Who:** separated/divorced parents sharing custody; often one organised parent and one reluctant one; lawyers and mediators as influencers.
2. **Frequency:** daily (messages, handovers) and weekly (schedule changes, expenses).
3. **Importance:** high — conflict reduction, documentation for court, money.
4. **What they use now:** OurFamilyWizard, TalkingParents, AppClose, 2houses, Custody X Change, BestInterest; otherwise texts, screenshots, spreadsheets.
5. **Cost of current solutions:** $110–299.88/yr per parent (OFW) — two parents pay $220–600/yr; TalkingParents $7–32/mo per account; AppClose $7.99 web / $8.99 in-app per parent; 2houses $169.99/yr per family (web) or $27.99/mo in-app; Custody X Change from $6/mo; BestInterest free tier then $9.99/mo+ (V/3P).
6. **Why insufficient:** Civly's analysis of 9,598 store reviews (3P): pricing ~1,200+ mentions, notifications that never arrive ~450+, reliability, support, calendars that cannot model real custody. Verbatims (quoted by Civly, 3P): "I can't afford it so I haven't talked to my kids." (TalkingParents); "I'd gladly pay monthly if it would be 98% reliable." (AppClose); "search does nothing but find you a local lawyer." (AppClose). OFW 1.3★ on SmartCustomer (111 reviews), TalkingParents 1.4★ (76) (3P via a competitor).
7. **Will they pay:** yes — when required by a court or clearly useful; but resentment in a captive market does not by itself create a reachable switching market.

## 5. Distribution and viral potential

- **Referral by professionals:** family lawyers, mediators, parenting coordinators, therapists — a slow but durable B2B2C channel; provide court-ready export templates and a "recommend to clients" kit.
- **Inherent invite loop:** the organised parent invites the other parent; the one-parent mode means the first parent gets value even before the invite is accepted, then nudges.
- **Community:** r/coparenting, r/Divorce, single-parent Facebook groups; content on "how to document for court", "custody calendar templates".
- **ASO:** "co-parenting app", "custody calendar", "co-parent messages" — moderate competition; incumbents' review volumes are 28–57K.
- **Shareability:** limited (private subject); value spreads by recommendation, not virality.
- **Paid:** Productivity-proxy CPI $3.58 → ≈$179/payer at 2% conversion vs $41.65 net on a $49 annual (red-team Calc) — paid acquisition only works if conversion is far above median (court-mandated users convert at very high rates, but reaching them is the problem).

---

## 6. Monetisation design

- **Model:** annual subscription, per family ($79/yr) or per parent ($49/yr) — explicitly attacking the per-parent double-billing resentment.
- **Free:** one-parent documentation mode with limited history and basic calendar (so the first parent gets value and the second parent has no excuse); watermarked exports.
- **Paid:** unlimited tamper-evident history, custody-rule engine, expense ledger with receipts and settlement, court-ready PDF exports, AI custody-order→calendar draft with review, tone-check, notifications with delivery receipts, multi-child, lawyer-share links.
- **Trial:** 14–30 days (court deadlines make longer trials sensible; RevenueCat: 17–32-day trials convert 42.5%).
- **Renewals:** multi-year plans (OFW sells 2-year); "your records" value recap; retention is structural until children age out.
- **Cancellation reduction:** records remain exportable; downgrade to read-only archive rather than delete.
- **Why subscription is justified:** continuous record-keeping and scheduling over years; one-off purchase would not cover storage, notifications and legal-template upkeep.

## 8. Product blueprint

**Positioning:** "The co-parenting record that works even when your ex won't — one price per family, court-ready from day one."

**USP:** one-parent mode, per-family pricing, custody rules that match real orders, reliable notifications with receipts, AI order-to-calendar with human review.

**Core functionality**
- Tamper-evident messaging (immutable log, timestamps, read receipts, no edits/deletes; export with hash)
- Custody calendar engine: alternating patterns, holiday overrides, exceptions, swaps with approval trail, multi-child
- Expense ledger: receipts, categories, 50/50 or % splits, settlement requests, running balance
- Document vault: orders, school, medical
- Court-ready exports (PDF with hash and timeline)
- **One-parent mode:** log external texts/emails via screenshot (OCR), document exchanges, generate records without the other parent's participation; invite flow when ready
- AI: custody-order upload → draft calendar (review required, every rule shown with its source clause); tone-check before send (suggest neutral rewording, never auto-change)
- Notifications with delivery receipts; weekly schedule digest

**Premium:** everything above beyond the free documentation tier; lawyer-share links; multi-year archive.

**Onboarding:** children + other parent (optional) → paste or upload order (optional) → draft calendar → review → first message or expense in <5 minutes.

**Main screens:** Calendar · Messages · Expenses · Records · Share/Export · Settings.

**Expansion:** mediator/lawyer dashboards (B2B), child-info sharing (medical/school), payment rails for expense settlement, UK/AU localisation.

## 9. Technical architecture

- Expo / React Native (New Architecture) + TypeScript; Supabase (Postgres, Auth, Storage, Realtime); append-only message tables with hashes; signed PDF exports; RevenueCat; PostHog + Sentry; Expo push with delivery tracking plus SMS fallback for critical notices.
- AI: Claude Sonnet 5.5 for custody-order parsing into a rule schema with cited clauses; Haiku 5.5 for tone suggestions; strict JSON; human confirmation gates; 5.1.2(i) consent.
- Security/privacy: minors' data; RLS per family; encryption at rest; export/delete; retain records on downgrade (read-only).
- **Difficulty:** medium–high (rule engine, admissibility expectations, notification reliability). **Time to MVP:** 4–6 weeks. **Backend complexity:** medium. **Platform limits:** push reliability on Android OEMs; legal admissibility varies by jurisdiction — never claim "court-admissible", say "court-ready export". **Maintenance:** holiday calendars per jurisdiction, template upkeep.

## 10. Risks and red-team

1. **Court-driven demand favours accepted incumbents.** The buyer may not choose on app quality; familiarity, records and legal workflows matter more than cheapness. Switching mid-case is unlikely.
2. **One-parent mode removes coordination friction by removing much of the coordination value.** If the other parent never joins, Google Calendar plus screenshots is the real competitor — the paid value must be the record and the exports.
3. **Custody-order parsing has asymmetric downside.** A missed exception, holiday rule or timezone can cause real conflict; verification and support costs could swamp a cheap subscription. Mandatory review, clause citation, and conservative defaults.
4. **Pricing resentment in a captive market does not establish a reachable switching market.**
5. **Mobile web would suffice** for calendars, messaging and records; native earns its keep through notification reliability — which is infrastructure, not a moat.
6. **AI genuinely helps** with draft extraction and tone, but must not imply legal interpretation or court acceptance.
7. **CAC:** $41.65 net on $49/yr vs ~$179 proxy CAC; lawyer referrals improve this but require trust and sales effort.
8. **Incumbent response:** OFW owns Cozi and can bundle family-organiser features; TalkingParents and AppClose already moved to paid-only, signalling they expect to retain users regardless.

**Red-team (GPT-6.1) verdict:** potentially excellent multi-year value *if* adopted by the relevant parties and reliable under high-stakes use; otherwise a hard-to-reach captive market. Not the first bet for a small budget.

- **Weeks 1–6:** MVP (messages, calendar engine, expenses, exports, one-parent mode); legal review of record-keeping claims and minors' data; jurisdiction holiday tables for 5 US states.
